Poetry is one of the oldest forms of human expression, existing before written language.  
---
The word “poetry” comes from the Greek poiesis, meaning “making” or “creating.”  
---
The earliest recorded poems include the Epic of Gilgamesh and Rigveda, both over 3000 years old.  
---
William Shakespeare wrote 154 sonnets, most exploring themes of time, beauty, love, and mortality.  
---
A sonnet traditionally has 14 lines and follows a fixed rhyme scheme.  
---
Haiku is a Japanese poetic form with 17 syllables in a 5-7-5 pattern.  
---
Blank verse is unrhymed iambic pentameter, famously used by Shakespeare and Milton.  
---
The Romantic poets believed that poetry should express emotion and imagination rather than reason.  
---
"Free verse" means poetry without a regular rhyme or meter, popularized in the 20th century.  
---
Emily Dickinson wrote nearly 1800 poems, most unpublished in her lifetime.  
---
An elegy is a poem written in memory of someone who has died.  
---
An ode is a lyrical poem expressing praise or admiration.  
---
Alliteration is the repetition of initial consonant sounds, as in “wild winds whisper.”  
---
Metaphor is a comparison that says one thing is another, deepening imagery and meaning.  
---
Modernist poets like T.S. Eliot and Ezra Pound experimented with structure and fragmented language.  
---
Spoken word poetry emphasizes performance, rhythm, and voice more than written form.  
---
Concrete poetry arranges words on the page to form a visual image.  
---
Epic poems like The Iliad or The Mahabharata tell long heroic tales in verse.  
---
Meter in poetry refers to the rhythmic structure, defined by stressed and unstressed syllables.  
---
Poetry’s power lies in condensation—using few words to evoke deep emotion or imagery.
